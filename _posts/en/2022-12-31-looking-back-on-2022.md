In 2022 I set a personal record: the longest continuous stretch in a single
city since I started university. For one reason and another it felt as though a
great many things could not be done — and yet a great many things happened.

Around the office, most of the restaurants either closed or changed hands, over
and over. The good news is that a few roadside places I like held on. Several
friends' companies expanded fast in 2021 and then had to face large layoffs in
2022. And one after another I kept hearing that people I had read about in books
and seen on television since childhood had died. In a year that so often fell
silent, that news stood out sharply. Some things changed for me too, by choice
and otherwise: more chances to sit still and go deep on the technical work, and
to think about creating value, and about the variables in a life.

## The work

I spent part of 2022 starting some genuinely interesting work. On the whole —
value produced, research results — call it 80 out of 100. Some projects slipped,
though I believe several important results will land in the first quarter of
2023. As always, it is a privilege to work with a group of people who are
single-minded about doing excellent work. It was a real shame that some
colleagues left the team this year; I hope everything goes well for them in
their new fields.

To do this new work properly I read a lot of papers across algorithms, biology,
chemistry, materials and engineering, and I do think I learned something. I
rethought where and in what form algorithms — data-driven AI methods in
particular — actually get to be applied. Generative models made enormous
progress in 2022: in painting, in conversation, AIGC gave people an explosively
large imaginative space. If you think carefully about how AI technology produces
commercial value, then rather than saying the best application is wherever large
volumes of data are generated, it may be that AI has more room to land where the
value of the data is *underestimated*.

I spent some time specifically on how to amplify the value of algorithms in
biology. The main thing I took away is: go with the grain. The complexity of
biological systems makes it very hard for us to design an entirely new system
ourselves. The better approach is to start from the data, from the standpoint of
the biological entity itself, build models that actually guide the work, and
develop against the rules biology already follows. Optimising on top of an
existing system is a far more efficient route from technology to result.

Finally I spent part of the year thinking about nonlinear growth — mainly, how
to find the crossing points of a core technology between fields that look
different but are in fact related, and how to design effective mechanisms for
exchange and collaboration that amplify the value released by trading technology
and data, so the whole grows quickly.

On the organisational side I tried IPD, gamification and so on, and read a
number of management books — only to find that at my current half-understanding
level, last year's economist-exam textbook suited me best. So I gathered up some
classic MBA texts and started reading. There is a line in Dalio's *Principles*
that is quite right: we fall into these holes only because we do not know the
history. A great many things simply recur, and if you study history seriously
you can know more of them.

By the end of 2022 I gradually felt I understood more about concepts I had
assumed I already had a grip on — and respected them more. Innovation, for
instance. Focus. The relationship between social impact and commercial
translation. Financial models. A lot of things have to be thought about from a
relaxed state; the thinking is like water held in your palms — you have to watch
it quietly, and if you are tense and grip too hard, no idea is left at all.

For 2023 I want to take the application directions I can now see and extend them
according to my own understanding, find their focal point, and concentrate on the
core technology. I will try to pay closer attention to landing applications, and
under a goal of long-term social value, build richer means and paths that reach
long-term goals through short-term results. I tried building some processes in
2022 but understand them only superficially. In 2023 I want to concentrate on
understanding one or two processes from zero to one, and see whether I can draw
out any regularities.

## Personal interests

I picked up a few skill points here and there this year. On the guqin I got as
far as *Ou Lu Wang Ji*. On the skateboard I can just about land a fakie 180 —
mostly a matter of not being bold enough, or I might have learned more. I
gradually got the drawing habit back, did some pencil studies and tried oils. I
did not hit the reading target I set at the start of the year, which may have
something to do with travelling less. I read an assortment of unrelated books.
In the first half of the year I finally finished several books on the *Yijing*
that I had never managed to get through. When bored I did some research into the
skeleton of the crucian carp; I may write that up later.

In games, besides continuing to explore Hyrule, the one that stayed with me was
playing six or seven endings of *Detroit: Become Human* — including nuking
Detroit. I finally reached the end of *Uncharted 4*, and played *A Way Out*.
*Death Stranding*, *Beyond*, *Nioh* (punishing), the *FFVII* remake and *Shadow
of the Colossus* are all still in progress. But the deepest impression was *Edith
Finch*: the fates of the family members, one after another, seem to keep
restating the difference between one individual existence and another, and the
hollowness of a universal set of values. That may mean rather more given how
2022 went.

In the last month of 2022 I started learning Spanish; I can now ask directions,
buy a ticket and order a meal. Also, I am better at lockpicking than I was in
2021, and I replaced both the inner tube and the tyre on my bicycle myself.

## Finally

Some things happened in 2022 that had a large impact and about which there was
nothing to be done. Those I will digest quietly, in my own time. Looking back at
what I expected at the start of the year, a great deal happened that I had not
expected. On the whole there were more shocks than pleasant surprises. Still,
it is 2023 now. I hope everything gets better in 2023, and that we become
braver.
